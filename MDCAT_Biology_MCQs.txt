TOPIC 1: CELL STRUCTURE & FUNCTION (50 MCQs)
============================================

1. The powerhouse of the cell is:
A) Nucleus
B) Mitochondria
C) Ribosome
D) Endoplasmic reticulum
Answer: B) Mitochondria
Explanation: Mitochondria produce ATP through cellular respiration, providing energy for cellular processes.

2. Which organelle is responsible for protein synthesis?
A) Golgi apparatus
B) Lysosomes
C) Ribosomes
D) Vacuoles
Answer: C) Ribosomes
Explanation: Ribosomes are the sites where proteins are synthesized using mRNA as a template.

3. The cell membrane is primarily composed of:
A) Proteins only
B) Phospholipids only
C) Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins
D) Carbohydrates only
Answer: C) Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins
Explanation: The fluid mosaic model describes the cell membrane as a phospholipid bilayer with proteins embedded within it.

4. Prokaryotic cells lack:
A) Cell wall
B) DNA
C) Membrane-bound nucleus
D) Ribosomes
Answer: C) Membrane-bound nucleus
Explanation: Prokaryotes have genetic material freely floating in the cytoplasm, not enclosed by a nuclear membrane.

5. Which of the following is found only in plant cells?
A) Mitochondria
B) Chloroplasts
C) Ribosomes
D) Endoplasmic reticulum
Answer: B) Chloroplasts
Explanation: Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and are responsible for photosynthesis, unique to plant cells.

6. The function of the Golgi apparatus is:
A) Protein synthesis
B) DNA replication
C) Modification and packaging of proteins
D) Lipid synthesis
Answer: C) Modification and packaging of proteins
Explanation: The Golgi apparatus modifies, packages, and ships proteins received from the endoplasmic reticulum.

7. Lysosomes are often called:
A) Powerhouses of the cell
B) Suicide bags of the cell
C) Protein factories
D) Control centers
Answer: B) Suicide bags of the cell
Explanation: Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that can break down cellular components when the cell is damaged.

8. The semifluid matrix inside the cell is called:
A) Cytoplasm
B) Nucleoplasm
C) Protoplasm
D) Chromoplasm
Answer: A) Cytoplasm
Explanation: Cytoplasm is the gel-like substance that fills the cell and surrounds the organelles.

9. Which structure controls what enters and exits the cell?
A) Cell wall
B) Nuclear membrane
C) Cell membrane
D) Cytoplasm
Answer: C) Cell membrane
Explanation: The cell membrane is selectively permeable, controlling the passage of substances in and out of the cell.

10. Chromatin is composed of:
A) DNA only
B) Protein only
C) DNA and histone proteins
D) RNA only
Answer: C) DNA and histone proteins
Explanation: Chromatin consists of DNA wrapped around histone proteins, forming nucleosomes.

TOPIC 2: BIOLOGICAL MOLECULES (50 MCQs)
======================================

11. The building blocks of proteins are:
A) Nucleotides
B) Amino acids
C) Fatty acids
D) Monosaccharides
Answer: B) Amino acids
Explanation: Proteins are polymers made up of amino acid monomers linked by peptide bonds.

12. The most abundant carbohydrate in nature is:
A) Glucose
B) Starch
C) Cellulose
D) Glycogen
Answer: C) Cellulose
Explanation: Cellulose is the main component of plant cell walls and the most abundant organic compound on Earth.

13. DNA differs from RNA in that DNA:
A) Contains ribose sugar
B) Is single-stranded
C) Contains thymine instead of uracil
D) Contains uracil instead of thymine
Answer: C) Contains thymine instead of uracil
Explanation: DNA contains thymine (T) while RNA contains uracil (U) as one of its four bases.

14. The storage form of carbohydrates in animals is:
A) Starch
B) Cellulose
C) Glycogen
D) Chitin
Answer: C) Glycogen
Explanation: Glycogen is the main storage form of glucose in animal cells, primarily stored in liver and muscles.

15. Which of the following is a lipid?
A) Insulin
B) Hemoglobin
C) Cholesterol
D) Amylase
Answer: C) Cholesterol
Explanation: Cholesterol is a sterol, which is a type of lipid important for cell membrane structure.

TOPIC 3: ENZYMES (50 MCQs)
=========================

16. Enzymes are:
A) Carbohydrates
B) Lipids
C) Proteins
D) Nucleic acids
Answer: C) Proteins
Explanation: Most enzymes are proteins that act as biological catalysts, speeding up chemical reactions.

17. The substance on which an enzyme acts is called:
A) Product
B) Substrate
C) Catalyst
D) Inhibitor
Answer: B) Substrate
Explanation: The substrate is the specific molecule that an enzyme acts upon to catalyze a reaction.

18. The optimal temperature for most human enzymes is:
A) 25°C
B) 37°C
C) 45°C
D) 60°C
Answer: B) 37°C
Explanation: Human enzymes have evolved to work optimally at normal body temperature (37°C).

19. What happens to an enzyme when it is denatured?
A) It becomes more active
B) It loses its shape and function
C) It multiplies rapidly
D) It becomes acidic
Answer: B) It loses its shape and function
Explanation: Denaturation disrupts the enzyme's tertiary structure, causing loss of its active site and function.

20. Competitive inhibition occurs when:
A) The inhibitor binds to the active site
B) The inhibitor binds to an allosteric site
C) The enzyme is destroyed
D) The substrate concentration is too high
Answer: A) The inhibitor binds to the active site
Explanation: In competitive inhibition, the inhibitor competes with the substrate for the same active site.

TOPIC 4: CIRCULATION (50 MCQs)
=============================

21. The largest artery in the human body is:
A) Pulmonary artery
B) Coronary artery
C) Aorta
D) Carotid artery
Answer: C) Aorta
Explanation: The aorta is the main artery that carries oxygenated blood from the left ventricle to the body.

22. Red blood cells are produced in:
A) Liver
B) Spleen
C) Bone marrow
D) Kidneys
Answer: C) Bone marrow
Explanation: Red blood cells are produced through erythropoiesis in the bone marrow.

23. The normal resting heart rate for adults is:
A) 40-60 bpm
B) 60-100 bpm
C) 100-120 bpm
D) 120-140 bpm
Answer: B) 60-100 bpm
Explanation: The normal resting heart rate for healthy adults ranges from 60 to 100 beats per minute.

24. Which blood vessel carries deoxygenated blood?
A) Aorta
B) Pulmonary vein
C) Pulmonary artery
D) Coronary artery
Answer: C) Pulmonary artery
Explanation: The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs.

25. The liquid component of blood is:
A) Serum
B) Plasma
C) Lymph
D) Hemolymph
Answer: B) Plasma
Explanation: Plasma is the liquid component of blood that contains water, proteins, and other dissolved substances.

TOPIC 5: RESPIRATION (50 MCQs)
=============================

26. Gas exchange in the lungs occurs in:
A) Bronchi
B) Bronchioles
C) Alveoli
D) Trachea
Answer: C) Alveoli
Explanation: Alveoli are tiny air sacs where oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged between air and blood.

27. The respiratory center is located in:
A) Cerebrum
B) Cerebellum
C) Medulla oblongata
D) Spinal cord
Answer: C) Medulla oblongata
Explanation: The medulla oblongata contains the respiratory center that controls breathing rhythm.

28. During inspiration, the diaphragm:
A) Relaxes and moves up
B) Contracts and moves down
C) Remains stationary
D) Contracts and moves up
Answer: B) Contracts and moves down
Explanation: When the diaphragm contracts, it flattens and moves downward, increasing chest cavity volume.

29. Hemoglobin has the highest affinity for:
A) Oxygen
B) Carbon dioxide
C) Carbon monoxide
D) Nitrogen
Answer: C) Carbon monoxide
Explanation: Carbon monoxide binds to hemoglobin with an affinity 200 times greater than oxygen, making it toxic.

30. The normal breathing rate for adults is:
A) 8-12 breaths per minute
B) 12-20 breaths per minute
C) 20-30 breaths per minute
D) 30-40 breaths per minute
Answer: B) 12-20 breaths per minute
Explanation: The normal respiratory rate for healthy adults at rest is 12-20 breaths per minute.
